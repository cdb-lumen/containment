The proposal correctly treats deliberate machine conversion as visual interpretation from the room brief rather than a newly discovered canonical event.

## Actual visible readability

- The lathe is distinguishable from a generic crate or console. Its chuck, stock axis, bed and carriage form a coherent machine silhouette. The guard has a visible break above the left housing.
- The manipulator is visibly articulated, with distinct joints and an anchored upright base on the right. Its gripper sits over the same horizontal workpiece as the lathe.
- Brown bands follow the left housing and fixed supports. Brown strands also track the arm. The exposed chuck face and ways remain visually separate from those bands.
- A cut dark cable with small exposed colored strands appears beneath the bed. This supports the deliberate-intervention idea when inspected closely, but is not a strong whole-board cue.
- The title, story explanation, player goal and concept-only disclaimer are legible in the whole-board inspection. Callout arrows identify the major parts without obscuring them. The source footer and palette labels are subordinate small text, not the main route to understanding the concept.
- The board presents a proposed converted state with explanatory "Before" and "What changed" text. It is not a before-and-after visual comparison or a demonstrated sequence of events.

## Defects and unresolved visual questions

1. Alien material is weaker than machine identity. The evenly repeated brown housing bands resemble straps or ribbing. The arm strands could be read as cables. Labels supply much of the alien interpretation. This does not block an annotated story concept, but it must not be counted as successful fibrous resin or tendon material work.
2. The workpiece support and tooling are schematic. The stock crosses the chuck, while the gripper sits above it and a large yellow form occupies its right end. The drawing does not make that yellow form's function clear or establish a convincing grip, suspension and cutting relationship. Resolve those relationships in the model rather than copying the diagram literally.
3. The small cut-insulation cue is easy to miss. Its visible location beneath the bed also needs a deliberate attachment and footprint decision later, so it does not become loose pickup-like floor clutter.
4. Chipped paint, asymmetric growth sockets, sparse wet edges and low work lamps are not convincingly demonstrated here. Flat yellow and brown areas communicate a palette, not finished surface treatment. No final material or lighting pass is warranted.

These are bounded concept limitations, not evidence that a production model or gameplay test failed. No such model or test was inspected.

- Make directional resin and tendon attachment visibly organic and asymmetric without burying the machine. Resolve insulation ends inside the intended machinery footprint and distinguish them from drops.

- Verify growth against authoritative collision. Record attackers along every legal lane and check small-enemy visibility, corners, projectile behavior and hazard readability. Do not infer these results from a clear side-view background.
- Review materials and local lamps in actual rendered frames. Retain oil-dark steel and chipped yellow, avoid poison-like green glow, and keep the combat floor quiet.
