## What works

- The upper exchanger skids and lower motor-pump skids form two recognizable paired installations around a low central service saddle. Wide outer routes and central bypasses remain visible. The arrangement is regular, but it reads as machinery rather than cylinders in a grid. Bolted exchanger heads, side expansion vessels, ribbed motors, exposed couplings and bent plumbing provide distinct functions and silhouettes.
- The separate saddle headers, paired transverse service covers and vertical runs to each installation suggest coherent supply and return routing. Pipes turn down within the skids instead of crossing walking space. This passes as a below-deck visual abstraction. It does not establish hydraulic continuity or distinguish flow direction at every connection.
- Desaturated turquoise enamel, dull metal, amber valve wheels and cool wall lights match the brief. Weathering is restrained. No steam or decorative liquid obscures the actors or the overview exit. The machinery bases appear grounded, with no obvious main-assembly clipping in either image.

## Defects and affected repair stages

1. **Stage4, pump and strainer readability.** In the desktop image, each pump casing presents as a tall rectangular turquoise block beside the coupling. Its curved volute profile is compressed out of recognition by the view. The strainer reads as a wire cage sitting on the rear pipe, without a clearly readable partial housing and exposed basket relationship. Source lines 74-111 establish that a shaped volute and half-cylinder strainer housing exist, but their presence in code does not resolve the pixels. Refine the casing silhouette and the housing/basket separation within the existing footprints so both forms read from the normal desktop camera. Preserve the successful motor, coupling and curved pipe cues.
2. **Stage3, story dressing.** The room has small colored indicator patches but no readable service labels or other specific indication that these pumps sustain ongoing life support and ship function. Nothing suggests dead passengers or an active purge, which is appropriate, but generic operating machinery alone does not deliver the brief's requested service-label cue. Add restrained, in-world service identification at a visible machine or the saddle. No new interaction or HUD change is needed.

No stage0-2 restart is indicated by these images. Preserve the paired layout, flush crossings and material palette.
